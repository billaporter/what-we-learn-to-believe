Terms of Use

2026
Walnut cane stand, aluminum white cane, printed signs Dimensions variable

Visual Description

Terms of Use is a sculptural installation featuring a white mobility cane displayed vertically in a dark walnut wooden stand, positioned between two framed interpretive signs. The installation rests on a rectangular, off-white pedestal in a gallery hallway, with glass walls behind it and a large concrete column to the right. The cane extends upward beyond the top of the photograph. Its white shaft has black sections near the handle and a red section near the bottom, ending in a white tip. A circular walnut base supports the cane, with a small wooden holder securing it partway up the shaft. On either side of the cane, white-framed signs are mounted on slender white stands with circular bases. Each sign contains a bold heading followed by a paragraph of interpretive text. The sign on the left is titled White Stick Thingy, while the sign on the right is titled White Cane. The two signs present contrasting definitions of the same object. The left describes the assumptions, stereotypes, and unsolicited interventions that Blind people may encounter, while the right defines the white cane as a practical tool for independent navigation, access, and autonomy. Interpretive Text White Stick Thingy An object carried by a person assumed to be helpless, dependent, confused, or in need of assistance. It may prompt unsolicited directions, physical intervention, excessive caution, pity, or suspicion. When its user acts independently, observers may question whether the person is truly blind. The object is commonly regarded as a symbol of blindness rather than a tool for independent navigation. White Cane A mobility tool used by Blind and low vision people to navigate independently. Through touch, it provides information about obstacles, terrain changes, and environmental features. Its distinctive white color also signals the user's visual disability to others, increasing awareness and safety in shared spaces. The white cane is an instrument of orientation, access, and autonomy.
